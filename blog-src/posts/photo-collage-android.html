A collage puts several photos in one picture. On Android you have two routes: the basic tool that may already be on your phone, or an app built for layouts.

The quick way: the tool you already have

Google Photos can make a collage: select a few photos, then look for Create and Collage. Samsung's Gallery app has its own collage option on Galaxy phones, under Create after you select photos.

Both are fine for a quick grid of a few pictures. They run out quickly when you want more photos, a shape like a heart, a set size for Instagram, or anything on the collage besides the photos.

The fuller way: a layout app

Collagio is a collage and carousel app for Android. It has more than 100 layouts for 1 to 9 photos, in five families:

  • Grid: rows, columns and mixed sizes.
  • Shapes: a heart, circle, diamond, hexagon or star over a grid of photos.
  • Diagonal: photos split along angled lines.
  • Curved: soft waves and arches between photos.
  • Freestyle: photos placed anywhere, at any angle, like a scrapbook page.

Step by step

  1. Open Collage on the home screen and pick your photos. You can select several at once.
  2. Choose a layout. Filter by how many photos it holds, from 1 to 9, and tap one to try it. Your photos drop straight in.
  3. Pick the format: 4:5 for an Instagram post, 1:1 for square, or 9:16 for a Story.
  4. Arrange. Drag a photo to move or zoom it inside its cell, swap photos between cells, and drag the borders between cells to resize them.
  5. Finish it. Set the border width, corner rounding and border color, then add a background, frames, stickers, text or a filter.
  6. Tap Save to phone. The collage saves as a full-quality JPEG, 1080 pixels wide, in Pictures > Collagio.

Collagio saves your project as you go, so you can close it and pick it up later from My projects.

Tips for a collage that looks put together

  • Choose a lead photo. Put your best shot in the biggest cell, or in the shape at the center of a shaped layout.
  • Mix close-ups and wide shots. A wide scene next to a detail is more interesting than six wide scenes.
  • Stick to a few colors. Photos that share a color look like a set. A filter on every photo helps when they don't.
  • Use the border on purpose. A thin white or cream border looks clean. No border reads as one picture. Rounded corners soften a busy grid.
  • Leave space for text if you add any, and check it on the full preview before you save.
